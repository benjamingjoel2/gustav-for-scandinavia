The best way from Oslo to Bergen takes seven hours. That's the point.

You can fly it in under an hour. Add the journey to the airport, security, waiting and the trip into town at the other end, and the difference shrinks to a few hours. What you lose in those few hours is the reason most people come to Norway in the first place: the landscape between the two cities.

## The line

The Bergen Line, Bergensbanen, runs about 500 kilometres between Oslo and Bergen. The journey takes roughly six and a half to seven hours, depending on the departure. Several trains run each day, plus a night train if you would rather sleep through it, though we wouldn't, at least not in daylight months.

It is one of the highest main-line railways in northern Europe. Its highest station is Finse, at about 1,222 metres above sea level. That doesn't sound high until you consider the latitude. At Finse the treeline is far below you. There is snow on the ground for much of the year, a glacier on the horizon, and a small cluster of buildings beside a lake. It feels much further from anywhere than it is.

## What you see

The first hours out of Oslo are gentle: farmland, lakes, birch and spruce, small towns with painted wooden houses. Around Hønefoss and through Hallingdal the valley narrows and the hills rise.

After Geilo the train climbs onto the Hardangervidda, one of the largest mountain plateaus in Europe. The forest thins, then disappears. For an hour or so the view is rock, water, snowfields and sky. In winter it is almost entirely white. In summer it is grey-green, with patches of snow that never quite melt. Cross-country skiers and hikers get off at small stations that seem to have no reason to exist.

Then the descent. The line drops through tunnels and snow sheds towards Myrdal and Voss, the landscape turning steep and green, with waterfalls on both sides. The last stretch into Bergen follows fjords and lakes. You arrive in the centre of the city, a short walk from Bryggen and the harbour.

## Which side to sit on

People ask, and the honest answer is that both sides are good. It is not worth worrying about. The landscape on this line is wide enough to see from either window, and you can always stand up.

## Breaking the journey

You don't have to do it in one go. Many people stop for a night, and it is worth considering.

**Finse** has a hotel beside the station. In summer it is a base for walks on the plateau and towards the Hardangerjøkulen glacier. In winter it is a base for skiing. There are no roads in. You arrive by train or on foot.

**Myrdal** is the junction for the Flåm Railway, a branch line that drops about 860 metres in roughly 20 kilometres to the village of [Flåm](/destinations/naeroyfjord) at the end of the Aurlandsfjord. From there you can take a boat through the Nærøyfjord and continue to Bergen by bus and train via Voss. This combination is popular in summer, and for good reason. It is also busy. We plan around the crowds where we can, with early departures and a night in the area rather than a rushed day trip.

**Voss** is a pleasant place to stop, with lakes, mountains and easy access to the surrounding valleys.

## Practical notes

Book ahead in summer and around holidays. The cheapest fares go early, and popular departures fill.

There is usually a café car or trolley service. The food is fine. A packed lunch from a good Oslo bakery is better.

Wi-Fi exists but is patchy in the mountains, which is not a bad thing.

Weather can affect the line in winter. Closures are rare but do happen during heavy storms on the plateau. Build in a little slack if you have a connection to make on the same day.

The night train is a sensible option outside the summer months, when much of the journey would be in the dark anyway. In June and July, take a day train. There is no reason to sleep through that light.

## The carbon question

Norwegian rail runs largely on electricity, and Norway's grid is mostly hydropower. Per passenger, the train is a fraction of the emissions of the short flight. We explain how we estimate this, and how uncertain those estimates are, on our page about [travelling lighter](/travelling-lighter). You can compare the two options for your own route in the [route builder](/route-builder).

It is not the main reason we recommend the train. The main reason is that it is a better day. But it is a good reason all the same.

## Why it wins

Flying from Oslo to Bergen is efficient. It moves you between two places. The train shows you what lies between them, and on this line what lies between them is a large part of what Norway is: valleys, plateau, ice, waterfalls, fjord. You arrive in Bergen having seen the country rather than having skipped it.

Seven hours, then. Bring a book you won't open.
