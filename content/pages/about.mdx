Gustav plans private journeys through Norway, Sweden, Denmark and Finland. Slowly, by train, boat and on foot, following the light.

## Why Gustav

Gustav is a name that Scandinavian kings carried for centuries, and one that is still at home in every Nordic country today. You will find a Gustav in a Stockholm office, on a Bergen fishing boat and in a Helsinki sauna. It is classic without being grand, warm without being familiar, and entirely unpretentious. That seemed about right.

## Gustav is not a person

There is no founder called Gustav. There is no fictional character behind the name either, no grandfather with a cabin and a story about the fjords. Gustav is the name of the company, and the company is a small team of people who plan journeys. We would rather tell you that plainly than invent someone.

## What we believe

Scandinavia is best experienced slowly. The light changes everything here, from month to month and from hour to hour, and the way to see it is to stay long enough to notice.

The journey between places matters as much as the places. The train across the Hardangervidda. The night ferry through the archipelago. The bus along the coast of Lofoten. These are not gaps between the good parts.

Fewer, longer stays are better than many short ones. For you, and for the places you visit.

Small and local is usually better than large and generic. The places we recommend are ones people from the area actually use.

Honesty is more useful than enthusiasm. We will tell you if your dates are wrong for the aurora, if a place is crowded in July, or if a route needs a flight.

## What we don't do

We don't run group tours.

We don't promise the northern lights, or whales, or clear weather. We put you where your chances are best.

We don't sell carbon offsets as a solution. Our approach to the environmental cost of travel is on the [travelling lighter](/travelling-lighter) page.

We don't use the names of businesses we haven't verified. Partners shown on this site are placeholders until they are confirmed.

We don't fill every hour. Some of the best moments on a journey are the unplanned ones.

## Follow the light

That is the whole idea, really. Come when the light suits what you want to see, stay long enough to notice it change, and travel in a way that lets you see the country between the stops.
