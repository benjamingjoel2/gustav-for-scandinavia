Travel has a cost. We can't make it disappear, and we won't claim to. What we can do is plan journeys that are lighter than they would otherwise be, explain how, and be clear about the limits.

## Flight-free by default, where it works

Within Scandinavia, we plan by train, boat and on foot wherever it makes sense. The rail network covers most of the region well. Night trains run from Stockholm to Narvik and from Helsinki to Rovaniemi. Ferries connect Copenhagen and Oslo, Stockholm and Helsinki or Turku, and Denmark and the Faroe Islands.

Sometimes a domestic flight is the only sensible option. Svalbard can only be reached by air. Some long journeys to the far north are only practical with a single flight. When that is the case, we tell you, and we show you the alternatives so you can decide.

You can read more in our journal piece on Scandinavia without flying, or explore the [Flight-Free Scandinavia](/journeys/flight-free-scandinavia) journey.

## The part we can't fix

If you fly long-haul to reach Scandinavia, that flight is likely to be most of your trip's footprint, often by a wide margin. A return flight from North America, Asia or Australia can outweigh everything else you do on the ground several times over.

We can't change that. What we can do is avoid adding to it unnecessarily once you are here, and suggest that if you are coming a long way, you stay longer rather than coming more often.

If you live in Europe or the UK, the picture is different. Reaching Scandinavia by train is realistic, and we will help you plan it.

## Why we don't sell offsets

We don't sell carbon offsets as a solution. Some offset projects do useful work, but the quality varies widely, and offsetting doesn't remove the emissions from a journey. It is too easy to treat it as permission to change nothing. If you choose to support climate projects, we would rather you do so as a contribution, not as a way to cancel out a flight.

## Ships are not low-carbon

Coastal voyages and cruises are a lovely way to see the Norwegian coast. They are not a low-carbon choice. Emissions per passenger on large ships can be comparable to, or higher than, flying the same distance, depending on the vessel and how it is run. Some newer ships use batteries or alternative fuels for parts of the route, which helps, but doesn't change the overall picture much.

We include coastal voyages when they are the right fit for a journey. We don't describe them as green.

## Small and local

We choose small, locally owned places to stay, eat and explore wherever we can: family-run guesthouses, neighbourhood restaurants, independent guides. More of the money stays in the communities you visit, and places of that size tend to have a lighter footprint than large resorts.

All of our partners named on this site are currently placeholders while we verify them. We will say so plainly when that changes.

## Slow means fewer, longer stays

The single most effective thing in a journey plan is usually the shape of it. Fewer stops, longer stays, less moving around. You spend more time in each place, see it more fully, and travel less in between. We think that makes for a better trip anyway.

## How the route builder estimates emissions

The [route builder](/route-builder) shows an approximate emissions figure for each leg of a journey. Here is how it works, and how much to trust it.

We use approximate per-passenger-kilometre factors for each mode of transport: train, night train, bus, car, ferry, coastal ship and flight. The flight factor includes an allowance for the non-CO2 effects of flying at altitude. They are based on the UK Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ) 2024 greenhouse gas conversion factors, which are widely used and publicly available.

Nordic rail runs largely on electricity, and the electricity in Norway, Sweden and Finland comes mostly from hydropower, wind and, in Sweden and Finland, nuclear. We adjust the rail factor down to reflect that, compared with the UK average. The adjustment is approximate.

Distances are estimated from the route, not measured precisely.

Real emissions vary a lot. For flights, aircraft type, load and route all matter. For ferries, the vessel, how full it is and how much freight it carries make a large difference. For trains, the actual source of the electricity at the time you travel. Our figures are averages and cannot reflect any of that precisely.

So please read the numbers as guides, not claims. They are useful for comparing options, for seeing that a train is usually a fraction of a flight on the same route. They are not a certified footprint for your trip.

## What you can do

Take the train where you can. Stay longer. Choose a journey that doesn't need a domestic flight. Pack lighter, which makes trains and ferries easier anyway. And if you have questions about any of this, [ask us](/enquire). We would rather answer them honestly than tell you what you want to hear.
